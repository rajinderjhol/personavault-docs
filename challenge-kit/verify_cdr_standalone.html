Nothing is uploaded. All checks run in this browser, with no network access, no account, and no trust in PersonaVault. Open the two supplied receipts: the valid one passes; the one-byte-tampered one fails at the content hash.

What this proves. The record's bytes are unchanged since the chair signed them; the chair's key produced the signature; the frozen DQI snapshot and any signed mandate or outcome receipt are bound to this exact decision.

What this does not prove. Integrity is not adequacy. This verifier does not re-execute the decision path, does not check that the recorded claim matches its source span or its operative order, does not check that consent was current, does not carry an independent receiver's attestation, and does not prove that no receipt is missing. It also does not prove clinical correctness, that the evidence was true, or that an outcome was accurately reported. Those are separate questions, and this page makes no claim about them.

Why the review panels are advisory. If a contradiction failed the receipt, an operator would be tempted to suppress it and lose the immutable record. Keeping the seal valid while flagging the contradiction preserves both: what happened stays sealed, and what is questionable stays visible to the human reviewer.

A concrete example in the supplied valid receipt. Its adopted item says “Recommend targeted therapy as the primary treatment option”, while the operative order names carboplatin, and the pre-AI commitment's structured plan is empty. All 16 checks still pass. That is the exact boundary between byte integrity and semantic adequacy — not a defect in the verifier, but a limit it states rather than hides.
